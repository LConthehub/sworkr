Get started

Sworkr runs on your own computer. There is no account with us and nothing is sent to us. Pick one way to run it.

Docker (recommended: Windows, Mac, Linux or a home server)

Never used Docker? That's fine: you won't need to type any commands.

  1. Install Docker Desktop, open it, and wait until it says Engine running. (More help with this step.)
  2. Download sworkr-start.zip and unzip it somewhere you'll keep it, like Documents.
  3. Windows: double-click Start sworkr. Mac: right-click Start sworkr.command, choose Open, then Open again (only the first time: the Mac asks because the file didn't come from the App Store). Linux: run ./start.sh.
  4. The first start downloads Sworkr (a few minutes), then your browser opens it.

The same folder has Update sworkr and Stop sworkr. Your records stay through both.

Prefer a terminal, or want a different port, your content folder or your own web address? Make your setup file: it's the same thing, with your choices in it.

Windows without Docker

  1. Download Sworkr-windows.zip from the home page and unzip it somewhere you'll keep it, like Documents\Sworkr.
  2. Double-click Sworkr. Windows may say it "protected your PC" because the app is new: choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Your browser opens Sworkr. A small icon appears by the clock: click it to open Sworkr again, open your backups folder, or quit.

Your records are kept in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Sworkr, your backups in Documents\Sworkr backups.

Your first minutes

Create your account. That first account is yours and is the admin. Use a password of 12 or more characters and keep it somewhere safe. Then tick the kinds of work you do, and Sworkr sets itself up to match (expense categories, workplaces and their usual charges). You can change it any time under Settings → My work. Prefer it dark? Settings → Look.

A new copy has a few made-up clients so you can look around. Remove them under Settings → Data & users → Example data.

Do these three things on day one

  1. Turn on backups. Settings → Data & users → Backups. Choose a passphrase and tick automatic daily backups. Write the passphrase down: without it nobody can read a backup, and neither can you.
  2. Turn on two-step sign-in. Settings → Security & privacy → Two-step sign-in, with an authenticator app. Save the recovery codes on paper.
  3. Encrypt your computer's disk (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on a Mac) and use a locked user account.

Your dashboard

Sworkr shows the parts that fit the kinds of work you ticked, and tucks the rest away (Settings → My work → Show everything brings it all back).

The first page shows what needs doing. Customize chooses the cards and their order. At the top, made for a phone:

  • Quick logs for your kind of work: one tap for a shift, a stream, calls, a scene or a sale. Add the hours and what came in, and it's in your income.
  • The safety timer: "tell my contact if I haven't tapped I'm safe in 20 minutes". For a walk home, a first meeting, anything.
  • What's running low from your supplies list.

Below that, besides bookings, tasks and money:

  • Routines: your own checklists (morning, night, before and after a booking), ticked off each day.
  • Notes: a scratch pad that saves itself.
  • Progress: private streaks, weekly goals you set, personal bests and badges. It never suggests working more, and Settings → Look turns it off.
  • Ask your numbers, if you turn on the assistant: questions about your own totals, answered by an AI model running on your own computer.

Updating

Docker: double-click Update sworkr in your sworkr folder (or run docker compose pull, then docker compose up -d there). Windows app: quit it, unzip the new version over the old folder, start it. Either way your records are upgraded by themselves, and a copy is saved first.
