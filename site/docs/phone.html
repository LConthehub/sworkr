On your phone

Sworkr only answers on the computer it runs on, on purpose. To use it from your phone without opening anything to the internet, link your phone and your computer with Tailscale, a free app that makes a private network between your own devices. Only devices signed in to your Tailscale account can reach Sworkr, and the connection is encrypted end to end. The same steps are in Sworkr under Settings → Connections → On your phone.

Set it up

  1. Install Tailscale on your computer and your phone (tailscale.com/download, or the App Store / Google Play). Sign in to the same account on both. Use an email you keep private.
  2. On your computer, open a terminal and run tailscale serve --bg 8765 (Windows app) or tailscale serve --bg 8008 (Docker). If it asks you to turn on HTTPS for your network, follow the link it gives.
  3. It prints a private address like https://your-computer.tail1234.ts.net. Open it on your phone and sign in.

Make it feel like an app

Do this from the address you'll really use: the Tailscale address (or your own web address), not localhost.

  • A plain name and icon: under Settings → Look → Home screen name & icon, choose a name like "Notes" and any picture. Phones that add Sworkr from then on show that instead of Sworkr. (Already on your home screen? Remove it and add it again.)
  • iPhone: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Android: in Chrome, open the menu and tap Add to Home screen.
  • Add a passkey on the phone (Settings → Security & privacy → Passkeys) so you sign in with your face or fingerprint.
  • Consider Away from home → passkeys only, so a password alone can't sign in from anywhere but your computer.

Android

  • Chrome offers Install app or Add to Home screen in its menu; Sworkr then opens in its own window, like on an iPhone.
  • The ntfy app works better on Android than on iPhone: it can show messages instantly even from your own ntfy server.
  • Tailscale and a VPN app can't both be on at once on most phones. Tailscale can send your internet through a VPN (an "exit node", or its Mullvad add-on) if you want both.
  • Passkeys are kept by your Google account (or your password manager); add one on the phone under Settings → Security & privacy → Passkeys.

Good to know

  • The computer or home server running Sworkr has to be on and awake. A NAS or small home server is always on, which is why it's a good home for Sworkr.
  • To stop it, run tailscale serve reset.
  • Don't forward a port on your router or point a web address at Sworkr by hand: it holds your most sensitive records. If you'd rather have your own address than Tailscale, follow Your own web address, which sets it up safely.
  • Tailscale's company coordinates the connection, but your data never goes through them. If you'd rather run that part yourself too, Headscale is a free replacement you can host at home.
  • For a message on your phone without opening Sworkr, see Notifications.
