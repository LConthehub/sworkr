Own your data

Sworkr runs on your computer so that your records are yours: no company holds them, reads them, sells them, or hands them over. The same idea works for much more of your life.

Why it matters for this work

  • Apps and payment services close accounts of people in this work without warning, and take the records with them.
  • Anything a company holds can be lost in a breach, sold, or handed over when asked. What's only on your computer can't be.
  • Your client list, your money and your health are the most private things you have. They're safest where only you hold the key.

Other things you can run yourself

All free, and all fine to set up later, one at a time. Each replaces a company that would otherwise hold that part of your life.

  • Nextcloud: files, photos, calendar and contacts (instead of iCloud or Google). Sworkr syncs its calendar and reads your content from it.
  • AdGuard Home: blocks ads and trackers for every device on your home network.
  • Vaultwarden: a password manager (works with the Bitwarden apps).
  • Immich: a photo library with phone backup, like Google Photos.
  • ntfy: notifications to your phone, from your own server.
  • A local AI (LM Studio, Ollama): Sworkr's assistant can use it, so your questions never leave home.
  • Headscale: run the part of Tailscale that connects your devices.

A small always-on computer (a NAS, a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5) can run all of these together, and Sworkr with them.

A VPN, and when it helps

A VPN hides what you do online from the network you're on, and hides where you are from the sites you visit. It's worth turning on:

  • On hotel, Airbnb, café and airport Wi-Fi, especially on tour.
  • When you look up a client, a venue or an address, so searches can't be tied back to your home.
  • When you post or log in to work accounts from somewhere you don't want linked to them.

Choose one that doesn't keep logs and lets you pay without your name (Mullvad and Proton VPN are widely trusted). On a phone, only one VPN-type app runs at a time, and Tailscale is one: use Tailscale's exit-node or Mullvad option to have both. A VPN can slow Docker's first download: pause it for that.

Small habits that add up

  • Go through Settings → Security & privacy → Privacy check-up once, and again every few months.
  • Keep a copy of your backups somewhere other than this computer (how).
  • Update Sworkr when there's a new version: double-click Update sworkr.
